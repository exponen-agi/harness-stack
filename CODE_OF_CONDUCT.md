# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be respectful. Assume good faith. Disagree about ideas, not people. That
covers almost every situation you'll run into here.

## The longer version

This project welcomes contributors regardless of experience level,
background, or how they found their way to AI-native engineering — including
people with **no prior AI experience at all**, which is exactly who this
project is written for. Nobody should feel talked down to for asking a
"basic" question, and nobody should feel dismissed for pointing out a rough
edge.

Examples of behaviour that helps:

- Being patient with beginners, including in issues, PR reviews, and
  discussions.
- Giving specific, actionable feedback instead of just "this is wrong."
- Assuming a mistake was a mistake, not bad faith.
- Focusing critique on the code, the docs, or the idea — never the person.

Examples of behaviour that isn't OK:

- Personal attacks, insults, or belittling comments.
- Harassment of any kind, public or private.
- Publishing someone else's private information without consent.
- Sustained disruption of issues, PRs, or discussions.

## Scope

This applies anywhere the project's community interacts: GitHub issues, pull
requests, discussions, and any other official space.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, please open a
confidential report via
[GitHub's report abuse form](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) or
contact the maintainer listed in [`.github/CODEOWNERS`](.github/CODEOWNERS)
directly. Reports will be handled discreetly, and reporters will not face
retaliation for reporting in good faith.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove comments, close issues/PRs, or (in serious or
repeated cases) block a contributor from the project, at their discretion,
to keep this a safe and welcoming space.

This document is intentionally short and plainly worded, in keeping with
this project's goal of being approachable to contributors without a
software-engineering or AI background. If anything here is unclear, that's
worth an issue on its own.
